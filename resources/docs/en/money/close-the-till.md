At the end of a shift or day, a cashier counts the cash in the drawer and closes the till. The system shows what it expected and keeps the result, so shortages and overages are visible. It is part of every plan.

## What the system expects

For each cashier, from their last closing (or the start of today for the first one) until now:

**Expected cash = float from last closing + cash received − cash refunded**

Only this cashier's own cash payments count (including debt payments they recorded), and only cash refunds on returns they recorded. Card, mobile money, cheque and bank payments are listed in the report but are not expected in the drawer.

## Close your till

1. Count the cash in the drawer.
2. In the side menu, under **Sales**, click **Till closing**, then **Close till**.
3. Check the figures: **Float from last closing**, **Cash received**, **Cash refunded** and **Expected cash**.
4. Type **Counted cash**.
5. Type **Float left in drawer**: the cash that stays for the next shift. The rest is taken out (**Cash taken out**). The float cannot be more than the counted cash.
6. Add a **Note** if there is something to explain, then click **Close till**.

The report page shows the **Difference** (counted minus expected). Red means short, amber means over, green means exact. Click **Print** to print it. You cannot change a closing afterwards.

The next closing starts where this one ended, with your float as the opening float, so no sale is counted twice or missed.

## Who sees what

- **Cashier**: closes their own till and sees only their own closings.
- **Owner and Manager**: can close too, and see everyone's closings with the cashier name.
- **Accountant**: can see everyone's closings but cannot close.

## What is not tracked

Cash taken out of the drawer for anything other than sales and refunds is not known to the system: paying a supplier, buying something small, or an expense. Such payments will show as a shortage of the same amount. Write the reason in the **Note**.

> **Tip:** Close the till at the same time every day, and never take money out of the drawer without writing it down.
